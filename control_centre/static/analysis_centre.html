Why groups get rejected

Every non-accepted row in group_log.csv, broken down by the stage that rejected it. Defaults to every area combined — pick one from the dropdown to see just its breakdown instead.

Private groups by area type

Every group in group_log.csv is matched to a rural/urban classification from mySociety's UK Composite Urban Rural Classification (uk/data/reference/rural_index.json) — constituencies matched directly by name, wards matched via their parent local authority (the index has no ward-level classification of its own). "Private" means eliminated at the public_filter stage — not marked public at scrape time. Shown as a rate (private eliminations ÷ all groups considered for that area type), not a raw count, since urban areas are searched far more heavily than rural ones and a raw count would just track search volume rather than anything about the areas themselves.

Group counts & activity by area type

Only accepted groups (the ones that actually made a final groups_*.csv) — how many an area type has, and how active they are, is naturally about the groups that made the list, not every candidate considered. Uses the same rural/urban classification as the section above. Member count and posting activity are only recorded for groups processed since that data started being tracked — an older, not-yet-reprocessed area still counts towards the group totals below but is excluded from the averages, rather than dragging them towards zero.

Area averages: members vs. posting activity

One dot per area (not per group — see the scatter above for that) — its own average member count and average posting activity across its accepted groups, colored the same way as the rural/urban section. Answers "which areas have unusually large or active groups", rather than the spread within a category.
